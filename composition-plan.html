Story: Fillmore Jazz Story. Drafted September 16, 2026.

Layer scale (from styles.css): --layer-base: 0, --layer-raised: 10, --layer-sticky: 20, --layer-overlay: 30. Decorative layers use a local -1 inside their parent's stacking context.

One row per positioned, layered, clipped, rounded, or shape-wrapped element.
Element Content purpose Normal-flow baseline Chosen technique and why Containing block / stacking context Fallback or disengagement Required test
.skip-link Keyboard users can skip past the header and story guide straight to the article. It's the first link on the page, so with no styling it just shows up as a normal link at the top. I will use position:absolute to keep it off-screen until it gets :focus-visible, then it will show at the top-left. Because it's absolute, showing it doesn't push anything else around. No positioned parent, so it uses the initial containing block. I will give it z-index:var(--layer-overlay), which is the top layer. That makes its own stacking context and keeps the sticky guide from covering it. With CSS turned off it's still a visible link at the top of the page. Tabbing in from the address bar at 320px, 1280px and 200% zoom. The link should show up, not be covered, and move focus to the article.
.issue-badge A small "1940s - 1960s" tag that dates the story at a glance. The lede will say the same thing, so nothing is lost if the badge is missed. With no styling it's just a line of text right after the lede. I will use position:absolute with inset-block-start and inset-inline-end to pin it to the header's top corner no matter how long the title is. I will add inline-end padding to the header so the title never runs under it. The header will be position:relative, so it's the containing block. I won't give the badge a z-index, so it doesn't make a new stacking context. It will still paint above the header text because it's positioned. Under 52rem I will turn the positioning off and it will sit in normal flow under the lede. I will check 320px, 768px, and 1280px, long title, and 200% zoom. The badge should never overlap the eyebrow or the title.
.story-guide In-page links that jump to each section of the story. With no styling it's just a block of links above the article. I will use position:sticky with inset-block-start:var(--space-sm) in the first grid column, plus align-self:start so it has room to travel. I'm picking sticky over fixed so it can't leave its section. I will wrap the guide and article in .story-layout so the guide stops when the article ends and never slides over the documentation section. z-index:var(--layer-sticky) will give it its own stacking context. It will only stick when the screen is at least 52rem wide and 32rem tall. Smaller or zoomed-in screens get a normal list so every link stays reachable. I will scroll top to bottom at 1280px. It should stick at the start, stop at the end of the article, and never cover the documentaion. I will click and tab through ever link and inspect the stacking context in DevTools.
.feature-figure img A 1942 photograph of Buchanan Street from Post Street, taken weeks before the removal changed who lived in the neighborhood. The caption carries the credit and a link to the archive. With no styling it's a plain rectangle image with the caption below it. I will use clip-path:polygon() to angle the corners like a torn gig poster. The image is a scan of an archival print, so the trim stays inside the white mount border around the photograph and never touches the photographic content. No more than about 4% comes off any edge, and the cut at the bottom right stays shallow so the WRA reference number stays readable. The file itself is uncropped and unaltered. The clip uses the image's own border box. clip-path will make a stacking context on the image only, so it won't need a z-index. I will wrap it in @supports, so browsers without support show the full rectangle. The fallback is the complete photo, and the caption sits outside the clipped image. I will turn clip-path off in DevTools and confirm the image and caption are still complete. I will also inspect the stacking context.
.pull-quote Pulls out a quote about the Fillmore scene, wiht the source credited and linked. With no styling it's a normal blockquote under the figure. I will pull it up with a negative margin-block-start so it overlaps into an empty padding band at the bottom of the figure. It only coverst empty space, and it stays inside the article column. It sits in normal flow inside article. I will add position:relative and z-index:var(--layer-raised) , which makes a stacking context that the decorative glyph can anchor to. Under 52 rem I will drop the negaitve margin so it sits in normal flow with no overlap. At 1280px I will confirm the caption is fully visible above the quote. I will double the paragraphs to check growth, click the source link, and inspect the stacking context.
.pull-quote::before A big decorative quotation mark behind the quote text. It's a decoration only, so no information lives in it. It doesn't render at all without CSS, which is fine since it's decorative. I will place it with position:absolute behind the text and set pointer-events:none so it can never block clicks on the source link. I will use an empty alt string in content so screen readers skip it. .pull-quote is both its containing block and its stacking context. z-index:-1 will paint it above the quote's background but under the quote's text. If it doesn't render, nothing is lost. The quote and the link still work on their own. I will click the source link right where the glyph sits to prove pointer-events:none works, and check the accessibility tree to confirm the glyph isn't announced.
.shape-record A decorative vinyl record graphic beside the legacy section. It's decoration, not documentation, so it makes no historical claim and needs no photo credit. With no styling it's a block image above the paragraphs, and the text reads at full width underneath. An inline SVG, so it can use my color tokens. I will use float:inline-start with shape-outside:circle(50%) and shape-margin so the text follows the record's actual round edge instead of a square box. The graphic really is a circle, so the shape matches the content. It floats inside its section, and the section uses display:flow-root to contain the float. No stacking context and no z-index needed. The float and shape only apply inside @supports and at 30rem and up. Otherwise the image sits above the text and everything still reads in order. I will turn shape-outside off and confirm the text still reads fine. I will check 320px and 1280px, and double the paragraphs to confirm the text clears the float and returns to full width.
Rounded surfaces .stroy-guide, .feature-figure, .pull-quote, .shape-record Softer corners group the panels and set them apart from body text. The 50% radius is what makes the record read as a disc. With no styling every box has square corners and reads exactly the same. I will use --radius-sm and --radius-md tokens so the rounding matches the rest of my system instead of being one-off values. Rounding doesn't position anything or create a stacking context. I'm not using overflow:hidden, so nothing gets clipped. If border-radius isn't supported, the corners are square and the content is identical. I will check the focus rings follow the rounded corners and stay visible, and that no text gets cut off at 200% zoom.
